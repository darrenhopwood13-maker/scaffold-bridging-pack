Scaffold bridging between two properties - design submission pack

14.00 m bridging scaffold carrying a working platform between two dwellings being re-roofed · Christchurch, Dorset BH23 · BNS-SC-001 Rev P01 · 11 October 2026

This is a submission for check and approval, not an approved design. A platform that bridges the gap between two separate structures is outside every TG20:21 and TG30:24 compliance sheet, so it has to be designed and independently checked by a competent temporary works designer to BS 5975-1:2024. The design and its check are theirs. Nothing is erected, and nothing is loaded, until a permit to load is issued.

The five numbers that decide this job

14.00 m run11 frames, 22 standards at 1.40 m centres
5.75 m deck3 lifts at 2.00 m, 1.35 m wide platform
17x short on wind270 kNm overturning against 16 kNm of self-weight
30 ties3.0 kN each, 50% of assumed capacity - structural, not restraint
20 t kentledgethe alternative, shown only to prove it is not practicable

Because the scaffold cannot stand up on its own, the ties are the design: most of them go into the adjoining property, so written consent is the critical path. Without it this concept does not stand, and the rethink happens before erection rather than on the morning.

Before any tube moves

  • Ties. Written consent from the adjoining owner for the ties and the access. Party Wall notice as applicable. This is the critical path.
  • Measure. Clear width wall face to wall face. Everything here is set from an assumed 1.50 m - the sheet dimension given was roof edge to roof edge, which is wider than the walls. If it is not 1.50 m, the bay spacing and the platform change.
  • Measure. Eaves height and the soffit and gutter projection, once the strip begins. One rafter settles the roof pitch.
  • Confirm. Ground under the legs; services overhead and underfoot; whether anything is to be netted or sheeted, because that decides the wind case.
Two things still open, and they matter: how the tile packs actually get onto the roofs (hi-ab straight to the roof, or landed in the bay), and the real weight of a delivered pack - the loading bay is designed and signed for 1.0 t placed, 12.3 kN design. If the packs are heavier, that figure changes.

What this pack is not

  • Not a design: no member size is signed off, and the bridging beam is explicitly a candidate pending the designer's own check.
  • Not a permit to load, and it cannot be turned into one.
  • Not a TG20:21 compliance sheet, and no number in it may be presented as one.
  • Not a survey: everything marked SURVEY or ASSUMED in SC-04 must be replaced with a real value.
